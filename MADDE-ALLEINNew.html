Normal mode: click or drag across the keys to play equally tempered notes. Portamento mode: F0 follows the horizontal position of the mouse or finger continuously. Formant Following moves each formant F1–F5 to the harmonic of F0 nearest its set value. Computer keys Z…/ and Q…] play several notes at once. ← / → held: the voice glides chromatically down/up at 4 semitones per second. Page Up / Page Down: jump one octave. Space turns sound on and off. Esc turns vibrato on and off (while selecting a tonic, Esc confirms the tonic instead). Click the Hz value to type any frequency (65.4–1568 Hz).

Live spectrum (0–8 kHz)

Dashed lines show the formants as applied. With Formant Following on, the small grey ticks at the top mark the set (target) values. Drag a line to change its frequency or its dot to change its strength. Scroll over a line to change its bandwidth.

Vowel space (F1 × F2)

Camera control

The camera is off.

Jaw opening controls F1, lip spreading controls F2 and turning your head changes pitch (up to ±6 octaves). Closing your eyes and lowering your eyebrows lower the volume. On the selected hand, spreading the fingers raises F3 and straightening them raises F4. Larynx: the dots drawn below the chin mark the neck. Place your index fingertip on the larynx and slide it down along the neck: F1 and F2 drop together, as when the vocal tract gets longer. When you remove the finger, the effect fades out. When the camera starts, hold a neutral face and a relaxed, half-open hand for 1.5 s. Video is processed locally.

Load a MIDI file (.mid) with one melody line. The notes are played by the same voice source, harmonic levels, vibrato and formant filters as the keyboard, and are shown on the piano. While it plays you can change the formants live (vowel buttons, F1×F2 graph, spectrum, formant sliders, camera). If the file has several tracks, the one with the most notes is used; overlapping notes are played as a legato line (last note wins). Notes outside C2–G6 are shifted by octaves. The sustained main voice is muted during playback.

Sing-along analysis

Camera mapping and calibration

Face → F1 / F2 (independent)

F1 – jaw opening

F2 – lip spreading

Head rotation → pitch

Maximum range ±72 semitones; pitch is limited to C2–G6.

Eyes + eyebrows → volume

Attenuation = weight × eye closure + (1 − weight) × brow lowering, both relative to the neutral face. The manual Volume slider stays as the upper limit.

Left hand → F3 / F4

Raw: —

F3 – finger spread (abduction)

F4 – finger extension

Separating F3 and F4

Spread and extension are joint angles, so they don't depend on hand position or distance.

Finger on neck → larynx lowering

Finger: —

The point where the index fingertip first touches the neck becomes the reference larynx position. Sliding down by a distance d (as a fraction of face height) lowers both formants: F1 × (1 − w1·L), F2 × (1 − w2·L), with L = min(max, k · (d − dead zone)). k is the calibration constant. Sliding back up moves the reference with you. Either hand can be used. The hand on the neck is ignored for F3/F4.

Computer keyboard

Up to 8 notes can sound at once. Note keys are ignored while you type in number fields. ← / → glide the main pitch chromatically (4 semitones/s); Page Up / Page Down jump one octave. Esc toggles vibrato.
