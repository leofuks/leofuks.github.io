F1 = mapping(A × mouth opening + B × jaw opening). Mouth opening is the inner-lip gap; jaw opening is the chin's downward displacement relative to stable upper-face landmarks. Draw across the graph to shape the base levels (thin bars). The vocal-tension slope is added on top of this shape at the source (thick outline = actual excitation). Harmonics above 40% of the sample rate are muted (red).

Source excitation – vocal tension & breathiness

Vocal tension (source spectral slope)

Breathiness (aspiration noise at the glottis)

Source = voiced harmonics + aspiration noise. Both are summed before the formant cascade, so the noise takes on the vocal-tract resonances. Tension changes the harmonic amplitudes themselves (−6, −4, −2, 0 dB/oct relative to the base shape) with smooth transitions.

Formants F1–F5 (frequency, bandwidth, Q, peak gain, range)

The five resonators are second-order peaking biquads connected in cascade, so the combined response is H₁·H₂·H₃·H₄·H₅ (dB values add). Q = F / BW. Bandwidth is the primary control and Q is recalculated; editing Q recalculates the bandwidth. Peak gain is independent of Q.

Formant clustering (singer's-formant region)

Detection: two neighbouring formants belong to a cluster when their distance is below tolerance + sensitivity × (BW₁+BW₂)/2. The natural boost comes from multiplying overlapping responses in the cascade. Enhancement is optional: an extra resonator centred on the cluster whose gain grows with the cluster's overlap. It is shown separately (violet curve) and capped.

Head rotation → pitch

Maximum range is ±72 semitones (6 octaves); the resulting pitch is limited to C2–G6.

Eyes + eyebrows → volume

Eye closure = eyelid gap / eye width; brow lowering = brow-to-eyelid distance / inter-ocular distance, both relative to the neutral face. Attenuation = weight × eye closure + (1 − weight) × brow lowering. The Volume slider stays as the upper limit.

F3 – finger spread (abduction)

F4 – finger extension

Separating F3 and F4

Spread is the average angle between neighbouring fingers in the palm plane; extension is the average straightening of the finger joints. Both are joint angles, so they don't depend on hand position, rotation or distance.
